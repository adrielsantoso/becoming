## Facts

### From Dowker's talk (chunk 1)

Timestamps are from the YouTube video. The transcript is machine-made and garbles names and numbers; the names below are corrected.

- **The question (18:31).** Does time physically pass, or is passage only how we experience it? Dowker calls it a current debate with no consensus.
- **C, the two sides (19:11 to 22:24).** Paul Davies: the flow of time is an illusion, a matter of psychology. John Norton: time really passes, independently of us. Dowker names the sides *being* (Parmenides) and *becoming* (the Buddhist doctrine of dependent origination, quoted from Stcherbatsky's *Buddhist Logic*).
- **The majority view (22:59).** In her own, admittedly anecdotal, experience most physicists and philosophers side with being: past, present and future are equally real. Sean Carroll's description of the universe as one four-dimensional thing is her example (24:20).
- **E, Newton's picture (26:07 to 35:10).** Snapshots of three-dimensional space, stacked in time order, make a four-dimensional block. One global time ticks the same for everything, so far-apart events can be simultaneous. This is what made Harrison's clock solve the longitude problem (11:11 to 17:49).
- **E, Einstein's picture (35:22 to 41:53).** Spacetime is made of point events, the limit of ever smaller and briefer fireworks. There is no three-dimensional space, no global time and no simultaneity. Time elapses only along world lines, and differently along different ones.
- **The image (44:02 to 46:15).** It feels as if a lit spot moves up my world line, marking now. General relativity has no such spots, and could not: there is no way to say where your spot is when mine is here.
- **C (46:31 to 48:24).** Whether the block holds events *happening* or only events *having happened* cannot be settled within general relativity. Dowker sides with becoming: time passes, and it is a physical process that general relativity does not do justice to.
- **C, the growing block (49:04).** What is called for is a growing block: the past fixed and real, the future yet to happen. The idea is associated with C. D. Broad.
- **E, why quantum gravity (50:05 to 51:22).** General relativity assumes matter behaves deterministically; quantum matter does not, and nobody knows how it gravitates.
- **C, causal sets (51:39 to 57:53).** Spacetime is atomic. The atoms are held together by causal order: some are before others, and some are unordered. Our observable universe would need roughly 10²⁴⁰ of them. The idea was proposed independently by 't Hooft, by Myrheim, and by Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin, and championed by Sorkin.
- **C, asynchronous becoming (57:22 to 59:46).** The atoms are born in a continual random process, in an order that is only partial. Two things are physically real: the atoms with their order, and the birth process. The occurrence of an event is the birth of its atoms.
- **Her summary (59:54).** Newton's time is about space, Einstein's about events, and the physics she hopes for is about process.
- **Stcherbatsky again (1:00:25).** The Buddhist philosophers held that causal laws hold the flashes of becoming together. Her moral: draw on all of human thought, not one tradition.
- **C, what is missing (Q&A, 1:06:53).** So far there is only a classical model of the growth. A quantum one that respects relativistic causality while allowing Bell-inequality violations has not been found.
- **C, direction (Q&A, 1:11:17).** Becoming has a built-in direction: the past exists and the future is open. Yet the finished spacetime could still look the same run backwards. The arrow of time and the direction of becoming are separate questions.

### What a spacetime atom is (question 3)

- **E (as a definition)** A causal set is a set of discrete elements with a partial order that is transitive, never circular, and locally finite: only finitely many elements lie between any two. *Dowker 2005, §2.1.*
- **C** Causal set theory proposes that spacetime *is* such a set, its elements the "atoms" of spacetime. *Dowker 2005, §1.* It is one proposal for quantum gravity among several, and untested.
- **C** Order gives the causal structure, and counting elements gives volume; together they are meant to recover the geometry. *Dowker 2005, §2.3.* Dowker 2014 heads a section "Atomicity unifies order and geometry".
- **C** The discreteness scale is taken to be of the order of the Planck scale. *Dowker 2005, §2.3.*
- **C** The "Hauptvermutung" (main conjecture): two continuum spacetimes that approximate the same causal set should be close to each other. It is hard even to state precisely. *Dowker 2005, §2.3.*

### How Α begins (question 2)

- **C** In the classical sequential growth models, the causal set grows one element at a time, each new element born to the future of, or unrelated to, those already there. *Rideout & Sorkin 1999, §1.1 and §3.*
- **C** The growth is not thought of as happening in time but as constituting it. *Rideout & Sorkin 1999, §1.1.* This supports "there is no before the first atom".
- **C** The growth sequence starts with a first element, "0". *Rideout & Sorkin 1999, §3.*
- **C** In these models growth never stops: every element has a descendant (a result of Henson's). *Dowker 2005, §3.4.*
- **C** One special case, transitive percolation, gives a cosmology that cycles through expansion and contraction back down to a single element. The authors say this case is not viable as quantum gravity. *Rideout & Sorkin 1999, §2.*

### Becoming (questions 1 and 4)

- **C** Dowker identifies the passage of time with the birth of spacetime atoms: the present is the process of birth, and being belongs to the past. She calls partly ordered growth "asynchronous becoming", a term she credits to Sorkin. *Dowker 2014, "Growing discrete spacetime".*
- **C** The order in which elements are born is physical only as far as it shows in the finished causal order. Elements that are unrelated have no physical birth order, and labels are pure gauge, the discrete analogue of general covariance. *Dowker 2014, "Growing discrete spacetime"; Rideout & Sorkin 1999, §3.* This is your open question 1.
- **C** Against the "Blockheads" (her term, after Earman), who would run the growth to infinity and treat the result as a static block: that makes a different physical theory, one that fits our experience less well. "The occurrence of an event is not the event itself." *Dowker 2014, Introduction and "Growing discrete spacetime".*
- **C** Dowker does not claim this settles it: it is not yet known how asynchronous becoming would show up in a quantum dynamics. Her final thoughts open with "a certain ambivalence". *Dowker 2014, "Growing discrete spacetime" and "Final Thoughts".*
- **C** Ellis and Rothman's crystallizing block: the past is fixed and the future does not yet exist physically. The present is where quantum indeterminacy largely turns into definite outcomes, and some patches of indeterminacy remain after it. *Ellis & Rothman 2010, Abstract, §1 and §2.*
- **C, the counterweight** Rovelli argues quantum gravity needs a picture without time. In general relativity, coordinate time is a label with no direct physical meaning, and mechanics is better read as relations between variables. *Rovelli 2009, Abstract, §III and §IV.* Time is recovered only statistically, as "thermal time", and its flow and irreversibility emerge at the thermodynamic level. *Rovelli 2009, §VI.*

### Where Α ends (the boundary with matter)

- **E** At very high temperatures the Higgs field's symmetry is restored (its expectation value is zero). As the universe cools it breaks, and the field takes a non-zero value. *PDG, Big-Bang Cosmology, §22.3.4.*
- **E** Near the Big Bang the temperature is so high that rest masses become irrelevant, and particles are effectively massless. *Penrose 2006, p. 2.*
- **E** Massless particles experience no passage of time. With only conformally invariant (massless) contents, the universe has no way of building a clock. *Penrose 2006, p. 2.* The next step, Penrose's cyclic cosmology, is **C**.
- **E, worked out** In the radiation era, age and temperature are tied by tT² = 2.4 / √N(T), with t in seconds and T in MeV (PDG, eq. 22.44). Between the W and Z masses and the Higgs mass, the PDG's table in §22.3.2 gives 4N(T) = 381, so N = 95.25. At T = 100 GeV that gives t ≈ 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ s. So Α ends about twenty-five trillionths of a second in. *This number is my calculation from the PDG formula, not a figure the PDG states.*
- **E** The CMB comes from "last scattering", at about 370,000 years. *PDG, §22.4.1.* That is well inside matter, not the boundary.

### Images (question 1)

- **C** A causal set sprinkled on an accelerating (de Sitter) universe forms a network with a power-law degree distribution and strong clustering, like the internet, social networks and functional brain networks. The authors suggest similar laws may govern complex networks and spacetime. *Krioukov et al. 2012, Abstract and §II.* A ready-made image: spacetime growing like a web.

### A testable bet

- **C** "Everpresent Λ": because volume is a count of elements, it fluctuates, and so does the cosmological constant, by about 1/√V, keeping it always of the order of the ambient density. *Ahmed, Dodelson, Greene & Sorkin 2004, §II.* The heuristic goes back to Sorkin in 1991, before the 1998 measurements of accelerating expansion. *Same paper, refs. 2 and 7.*
